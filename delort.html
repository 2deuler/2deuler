Delort’s vortex-sheet problem

Existence of weak 2D Euler solutions for sign-changing vortex-sheet data

The problem

Delort’s discussion

The two-dimensional Euler equations describe the motion of an ideal incompressible fluid:

∂tu+div(u⊗u)+∇p=0,divu=0.

A vortex sheet concentrates vorticity on a curve. Delort proved global weak existence for compactly supported vorticity in H−1 that is a measure of one sign. He concluded with the following discussion.

The sign condition provides the compactness needed to pass to the nonlinear Euler equation. For general signed measures, that mechanism can fail. The manuscript proposes to address Delort’s question by constructing the solutions directly, for arbitrary finite signed initial vorticity in H−1, without requiring compact support.

The manuscript’s main claim

No restriction on the initial sign

Write ℳ(ℝ2) for the finite signed Radon measures and set

u0=∇⊥Δ−1ω0.

Theorem 1.1 · As stated in the AI-assisted manuscript

For every ω0 ∈ ℳ(ℝ2) ∩ H−1(ℝ2), there are infinitely many distinct global weak velocity solutions with initial value u0. They satisfy

u−u0∈C([0,∞);L2),(u−u0)(0)=0.

The vorticity ω = curl u satisfies ω(0) = ω0 and the following regularity properties. All spatial function spaces below are over ℝ2.

ω∈Cw∗([0,∞);ℳ)∩C([0,∞);H−1).
ω∈C((0,∞);L1).

Here weak-* continuity is tested against functions in C0(ℝ2). For ω0 ∈ L1, one also has ω ∈ C([0, ∞); L1). For singular initial data, continuity in total variation at zero is not asserted.

The approach uses convex integration in the style of Bruè–Colombo–Kumar: begin with an approximate Euler flow and remove its equation error through the time-averaged effect of moving dipoles. The corrections remain summable both in velocity L2 and in vorticity L1.

Scope

The solution class matters

The result concerns weak velocity solutions. It does not assert an energy inequality, preservation of the sign of vorticity, propagation of higher Lp norms, or renormalized transport.

It also does not identify a vanishing-viscosity limit or a physically selected continuation of a vortex sheet. In particular, the nonuniqueness statement is not a nonuniqueness result within the bounded-vorticity class of Yudovich.

Our purpose

We want to understand new arguments for the existence of weak Euler solutions with sign-changing vortex-sheet data: identify their main mechanisms, examine what they imply, make the ideas accessible, and explore their limits.

This page brings together the AI-assisted manuscript, its source material, and our efforts to interpret and explain it. We hope to collect comments, questions, and expositions that can be read alongside the original argument. We aim for transparency and to share the relevant AI interactions whenever feasible. Over time, we hope to complement the AI-assisted work with human expositions.

Invitation

Everyone is invited to collaborate and discuss. If you would like to examine an argument, point out a gap, contribute an exposition, or exchange ideas, please contact josef.demmel@fau.de. Questions, corrections, and comments are very welcome.
