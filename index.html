Two-dimensional Euler equations

Global weak Euler flows with finite signed initial vorticity

A construction of global weak flows from arbitrary finite signed vorticity in H−1, with a strong initial velocity trace and integrable vorticity at every positive time.

Read the paper ↗ Explore the main result ↓

01 / The problem

Starting from a vortex sheet

The two-dimensional Euler equations describe the motion of an ideal incompressible fluid:

∂tu+div(u⊗u)+∇p=0,divu=0.

A vortex sheet concentrates vorticity on a curve. More generally, vorticity may be a finite measure, with positive and negative parts. For such initial data, the difficulty is to construct a velocity that satisfies the nonlinear equation while retaining control of its vorticity.

This paper takes a constructive route: begin with an approximate Euler flow, then remove its equation error using moving dipoles. The corrections remain summable both in velocity L2 and in vorticity L1.

02 / The main result

No restriction on the initial sign

Write ℳ(ℝ2) for the finite signed Radon measures and use the inhomogeneous space H−1(ℝ2). The initial velocity is given by the Biot–Savart law:

u0=K∗μ0,K(x)=(−x2,x1)2π|x|2,x≠0.

Theorem 1.1 · Manuscript

For every μ0 ∈ ℳ(ℝ2) ∩ H−1(ℝ2), there are infinitely many distinct global weak velocity solutions with initial value u0. They satisfy

u−u0∈C([0,∞);L2),(u−u0)(0)=0.

The vorticity μ = curl u is weakly-* continuous as a finite measure and strongly continuous in H−1, with μ(0) = μ0. At every positive time it is an L1 density, continuously depending on time in that norm.

  • Strong initial trace

    The difference u(t) − u0 tends to zero in global L2. The initial velocity itself need not have finite global kinetic energy.

  • Continuity in L1

    When the initial vorticity has an L1 density, strong L1 continuity extends all the way to the initial time.

  • Circulation conserved

    The Biot–Savart identity holds at every time, and μ(t)(ℝ2) = μ0(ℝ2). Nonzero circulation is allowed.

Read the precise vorticity regularity

All spatial function spaces below are over ℝ2.

μ∈Cw∗([0,∞);ℳ)∩C([0,∞);H−1).
μ∈C((0,∞);L1).

Here weak-* continuity is tested against functions in C0(ℝ2). For μ0 ∈ L1, one also has μ ∈ C([0, ∞); L1). For singular initial data, continuity in total variation at zero is not asserted.

03 / The construction

Approximate. Repair. Pass to the limit.

The argument separates the initial trace from the removal of the equation error. Each repair keeps the velocity fixed at the endpoints of its time interval.

  1. Build the initial approximation

    Smooth the initial vorticity at successively smaller scales and evolve the approximations by exact Euler flows. Join them at carefully chosen times so that both the velocity error and the Reynolds stress vanish at the initial time. Section 3.

  2. Repair the stress with moving dipoles

    Approximate the stress by constant matrices on rectangles. A concentrated dipole visits their rows in succession. Its force, averaged over a complete cycle, supplies the required stress cancellation. A time correction vanishes at both ends of each cycle. Sections 4–5.

  3. Iterate and join the intervals

    Repeat the repair on dyadic time intervals. The corrections are summable in velocity L2 and vorticity L1. The limit solves Euler, and the fixed endpoint velocities allow the intervals to join while retaining the initial trace. Section 6.

The construction adapts the moving-dipole and time-averaging mechanism of Bruè, Colombo and Kumar, as discussed in the manuscript’s introduction.

Why infinitely many solutions?

Change the starting approximate flow near t = 1 before repairing it. Because the repair preserves the velocity at that time, the resulting solutions remain distinct. This is the separation argument in Section 7.

04 / Scope

The solution class matters

The result concerns weak velocity solutions. It does not assert an energy inequality, preservation of the sign of vorticity, propagation of higher Lp norms, or renormalized transport.

It also does not identify a vanishing-viscosity limit or a physically selected continuation of a vortex sheet. In particular, the nonuniqueness statement is not a nonuniqueness result within the bounded-vorticity class of Yudovich.
